### Reading Practice Plan (Ages 5–8)

**Goal:** Read a short story aloud with good fluency and answer simple questions about it by the end of 2 weeks.

---

#### Week 1 – Building Confidence

| Day | Activity (10‑15 min) | What to Do | How to Check |
|-----|----------------------|------------|--------------|
| Mon | Choose a picture book | Pick a book with big pictures and 20‑30 words per page. | Ask a parent to see the book title.
| Tue | Look‑around preview | Flip through the pages. Name the main characters and setting. | Say the names out loud; parent repeats back.
| Wed | Letter‑sound warm‑up | Practice the sounds of 5 new letters (e.g., *b, m, s, t, r*). Say each sound, then say a word that starts with it. | Parent checks you said the correct sound.
| Thu | Read page 1 together | Read the first page slowly, pointing to each word. | Parent counts how many words you read correctly.
| Fri | Fun finger‑trace | Trace the printed words with your finger while saying them. | Parent watches the tracing and listens.
| Sat | Story‑time with a sibling | Tell the story to a younger sibling using the pictures. | Sibling smiles or repeats a word back.
| Sun | Review & reward | Talk about the favorite part of the story. Get a sticker for the week. | Show the sticker chart.

---

#### Week 2 – Gaining Fluency

| Day | Activity (15‑20 min) | What to Do | How to Check |
|-----|----------------------|------------|--------------|
| Mon | Read a new page | Choose page 2‑3. Read aloud without stopping. | Parent notes how many times you pause.
| Tue | Word‑hunt game | Find 3 words that start with the same letter on the page. | List the words for the parent.
| Wed | Express emotions | Say how a character feels (happy, sad, surprised). | Parent asks a simple question: *“How does the cat feel?”* 
| Thu | Reread favorite page | Read the page you liked most twice. | Parent counts how many words you read correctly the second time.
| Fri | Draw & describe | Draw a picture of the story’s scene and tell the story in your own words. | Show the drawing and listen to your description.
| Sat | Quiz time (very short) | Answer 2 easy questions: *Who?* *What?* *Where?* | Parent asks and marks correct answers.
| Sun | Celebrate progress | Read the whole short story from start to finish. Receive a small prize. | Parent watches the full reading and gives praise.

---

### How to Track Progress
1. **Sticker Chart** – One sticker for each day you finish the activity.
2. **Parent Checklist** – Simple table (like the one above) where the parent puts a ✔️ when you complete the step.
3. **Weekly Talk** – At the end of each week, talk about what was easy and what was hard. Write down one new word you learned.

### Tips for Success
- Keep sessions short (no more than 20 minutes) so you stay interested.
- Use a happy voice and point to words; the pictures help you remember.
- Celebrate every effort, even if you make a mistake.
- If a word is tricky, say it slowly, clap the syllables, then try again.

You’re doing great! Keep reading and have fun with the stories.

### Why Is the Sky Blue? – Simple Science Plan (Ages 5‑8)

**Goal:** Explain in your own words why the sky looks blue, using a short picture‑book and a fun experiment, by the end of one week.

---

#### Day 1 – Talk & Picture (10 min)
- **What to do:** Look at a picture of a blue sky in a storybook.
- **Say:** “The sky is blue because…?”
- **Check:** Parent asks you to point to the sky and say the word “blue.”

#### Day 2 – Light Play (15 min)
- **What to do:** Shine a flashlight through a clear glass of water with a little milk mixed in (just a drop).
- **Observe:** Notice the light looks a little blue on the other side.
- **Say:** “The light is blue here.”
- **Check:** Parent asks you to name the colour you see.

#### Day 3 – Story Time (10 min)
- **What to do:** Read a short picture‑book that talks about the sky (e.g., *“Why Is the Sky Blue?”*). Read one page at a time, pointing to the words.
- **Say:** After each page, repeat the sentence “The sky is blue.”
- **Check:** Parent counts how many times you say the sentence correctly.

#### Day 4 – Simple Explanation (10 min)
- **What to do:** Using the words you learned, tell a grown‑up:
  - “The Sun sends light.
  - Light has many colours.
  - The blue part spreads out the most, so we see blue.
- **Check:** Grown‑up repeats back the three short facts.

#### Day 5 – Draw the Sky (15 min)
- **What to do:** Color a big picture of the sky with a blue crayon.
- **Say:** “I colored the sky blue because the sky is blue.”
- **Check:** Parent asks you why you chose blue and listens to your answer.

#### Day 6 – Review Game (10 min)
- **What to do:** Play a quick “ask‑and‑answer” game. Parent asks, “Why is the sky blue?” You answer in one sentence.
- **Check:** Parent gives a happy sticker if you answer with the three facts.

#### Day 7 – Celebration (5 min)
- **What to do:** Tell a sibling or friend the story you learned.
- **Check:** You get a high‑five for sharing!

---

**How to Track Progress**
- Keep a simple chart with a smiley face for each day you finished the activity.
- After Day 7, look at the chart. If you have at least 5 smiley faces, you’ve mastered the idea.

**Remember:** It’s okay to ask “why?” again. Every question helps you learn more!

---

**Extra Fun (Optional)**
- Watch a short cartoon about light and colour (2‑minute video). Talk about what you saw.

Good luck, little scientist! 🌈

We can learn about why the sky is blue by looking at how light moves through the air.

1. **Sunlight is a mix of many colours** – it looks white, but if you shine it through a prism it splits into a rainbow.
2. **The air is full of tiny particles** – like dust and water droplets.
3. **Blue light bends more than other colours** – when it hits those tiny particles, it scatters in all directions.
4. **We see that scattered blue everywhere** – that’s why the sky looks blue to us.

Think about the blue sky like a big bowl of soup that is stirred with many colours of pepper. The blue pepper spreads everywhere, so the soup looks blue.
